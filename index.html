Research

Resistive switching in SiOx (B.Sc. thesis, BME, 2024 to 2026)

My thesis in the Halbritter group is about how a thin silicon oxide film changes its resistance when you push current through it. I am testing one picture, where conducting paths come from oxygen vacancies percolating through the oxide, against a more filament-like one, and comparing both with the resistance noise measured in the lab.

The simulation chain starts with a melt-quenched glass in LAMMPS with a machine-learned potential. On top of that sits a resistor network on the vacancy sites, and on top of that a kinetic Monte Carlo model of filament growth with Joule heating.

It is still early. One result so far: for one choice of barrier parameters I ran 30 seeds at each of 7 bias levels. Filaments grew at every bias, but only the low-bias ones survived. At 0.5 to 1.0 V all 30 runs ended with a stable filament, and at 2.5 V and above none did. The model does not match the measured noise well yet. It is off by about as much as the simpler static fit it was built from (R² of 0.53), so that gap is not new.

Generative LEGO (2025, restarted 2026)

Text or a photo in, a buildable LEGO model out: parts list, structure, instructions. It builds on BrickNet from CVPR 2026. I first tried this in 2025 and restarted it this year, with LLM-generated captions and, in the last few weeks, diffusion-style generation.

The results are not good yet. The best recipe is still the unmodified BrickNet checkpoint with best-of-8 sampling, a judge that picks between candidates, and a physical-validity check. A fine-tuned model I had reported as better did not survive a re-check, so I retracted it. Nothing in the pipeline yet checks whether a generated model would stand up under gravity. The research log has every number and every mistake.

Lilith (BadCompany, CTO, 2025 to 2026)

Lilith was a security layer for AI agents. An agent that can call tools can be talked into things you did not intend, and checking inside the agent does not help, because the agent is the thing under attack. So the rules lived outside it. Every tool call was checked against a policy written in Cedar, and on Linux the same policy was enforced in the kernel, so a compromised agent still could not open files or reach hosts it was not allowed to. We delivered it in 2025 and 2026, wrote it up on badcompany.xyz, and the project is finished.

gpu-waterz (CUDA)

A GPU version of waterz, the tool connectomics pipelines use to turn predicted affinities into labelled cells. I built it so that large volumes fit on a 24 GB 3090, then made it fast, and I wanted a CPU alternative as well. On a 5090 it segments a 2.16 billion voxel volume in 3.1 seconds, with segmentation quality (VOI) within 0.02 of stock waterz. Merging is a parallel approximation of the sequential algorithm.

The hardest part was getting the same answer twice. Agglomeration merges regions by the mean of the affinities on their shared boundary, and I summed those with float atomic adds. Float addition is not associative and the GPU adds in whatever order threads arrive, so two identical runs disagreed on about 740,000 of 7.5 million edges, which flips merge decisions and changes the labels. I switched to fixed-point integer accumulation. Integer addition does not care about order, and now the output is byte-identical from run to run.

lean-interval-bounds (Lean 4)

Tactics that prove bounds on exp, log, sin and cos in Lean 4 with Mathlib. There is an approx tactic and a norm_num extension, and every result is checked by the Lean kernel, so you do not have to trust any floating point.
